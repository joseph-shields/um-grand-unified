## The Two-Pole Structure in Ethics

Every ethical system has rules: deontological duties, categorical imperatives, prohibitions that contract the space of permissible action to a defined core. Every ethical system has consequences: the outcomes of actions, expanding into an uncertain future, context-dependent, variable. These are the two poles. Virtue — practical wisdom, the capacity to navigate between fixed rules and uncertain outcomes — is not a third independent ethical object. It is the forced interface that must exist wherever deontological duties and consequentialist outcomes coexist as joint constraints on action. You cannot have both binding rules and uncertain consequences without a mechanism for acting well in their presence; that mechanism is virtue. W_B is not a compromise between W_M and W_L. It is the forced structure of the interface between them.

The UM axiom is φ² = φ + 1, giving r = 1/(2φ) ≈ 0.30902 as the unique stable fixed point of x_{n+1} = 1/(4x + 2). Moral equilibrium is the fixed point that practical wisdom converges to.

## Key Results

- **STRUCTURAL:** Deontological ethics (Kant, natural law) is W_M-dominated: the rules are fixed, context-independent, applying regardless of consequences. Pure deontology sets x → 0, contracting all of ethics into W_M and leaving no room for W_B or W_L. This is a coherent ethical position but it is not at equilibrium: it suppresses the forced interface between rules and consequences.
- **STRUCTURAL:** Consequentialism (utilitarianism, expected utility) is W_L-dominated: outcomes are all that matter, expanding the ethical calculation to include all possible consequences. Pure consequentialism sets x → ∞ (or x → 1), expanding W_L without bound and leaving the deontological core (W_M) with negligible weight. This too is not at equilibrium: it suppresses the fixed duties that anchor moral reasoning.
- **DERIVED:** Kant's categorical imperative — act only on maxims you could will to be universal laws — is the fixed-point condition 4r² + 2r − 1 = 0 applied to moral maxims. A maxim is universalizable if and only if it is consistent with the fixed point: universalizability is the condition that applying the maxim to everyone does not destabilize the moral equilibrium. Non-universalizable maxims are those that would drive x away from r if universally adopted.
- **STRUCTURAL:** Virtue ethics (Aristotle, MacIntyre) is the correct characterization of W_B: the virtuous person is one who has calibrated practical wisdom to navigate between fixed duties (W_M) and uncertain outcomes (W_L) in a way that converges to r. Virtues are the stable W_B crossing rules — they specify how to traverse the interface between rules and consequences without losing either.
- **STRUCTURAL:** The moral floor ε_floor = r³ ≈ 2.95% is the minimum ethical cost of any action affecting another person. No action that has a moral dimension can be costless: even the most beneficial action incurs at least r³ in irreversible W_B crossing cost. This is the UM statement of moral realism: ethical actions are not free, and the minimum cost is not zero.

## The Fixed Point

In ethics, r ≈ 0.309 is moral equilibrium — the disposition from which neither deontological purity nor consequentialist maximization is a unilateral improvement. At r, the three channels are in their equilibrium proportions: W_M ≈ 9.55% (the non-negotiable duties), W_B ≈ 42.71% (the active exercise of practical wisdom), and W_L ≈ 47.75% (the consequentialist calculation over outcomes). Convergence to r is moral development: the process by which a moral agent moves from rigidity (rule-following without wisdom, x < r) or from pure outcome-chasing (consequentialism without constraints, x > r) toward the stable disposition of the virtuous person. The eigenvalue −4r² ≈ −0.382 governs the rate at which moral education corrects ethical deviation.

## The Floor

The braiding floor ε_floor = r³ ≈ 2.95% is the moral minimum — the floor below which no action is permissible regardless of consequences. This is the UM formalization of the deontological insight: there are some things that cannot be done no matter how good the outcomes, because the W_B crossing cost is always at least r³, and certain actions require W_B crossings that destroy the interface itself (e.g., torture, which destroys the victim's capacity to be a party to the W_B interface). For an action affecting n other persons through n W_B crossings, the accumulated moral cost is n × r³, setting the minimum moral weight of any policy affecting large populations.

## What Remains Open

The derivation of the categorical imperative from the fixed-point condition 4r² + 2r − 1 = 0 requires a formal mapping from moral maxims to parameter values in the UM map — which is not yet specified. The claim that virtue ethics correctly characterizes W_B (rather than, say, contractualism) is structural and requires a more careful analysis of alternative ethical theories. The moral floor r³ needs to be connected to specific moral intuitions or legal thresholds (e.g., minimum standards of due process, prohibitions on cruel treatment) to become testable.
